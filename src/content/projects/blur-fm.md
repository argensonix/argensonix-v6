In 2009, between exams, I plugged a desktop into the wall and started
broadcasting. A few months later it was Radio deUNA! — an interactive online
radio with on-demand requests. Today it is Blur FM: a place for those looking
to discover new sounds.

A radio held together by code — schedules, request queues and now-playing
metadata, all running on a small homegrown stack.
